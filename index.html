SAFETY FIRST

THE GOLDEN RULE

Never trust a mystery bin.

If you're unsure what something contains, pause before you sort it.

  • 01Use gloves for unknown or sharp objects.
  • 02Never puncture, mix or open mystery containers.
  • 03Keep batteries and chemicals away from regular bins.

A FIELD GUIDE FOR EVERYDAY SORTING

When in doubt,
start here.

A few quick cues for the things that don't always come with instructions.

One reminder for every streamEmpty, clean and dry your recyclables before they go in the bin.
01 · RETURNS TO NATUREBiodegradableQuick cues+

Food scraps, fruit and vegetable peels, coffee grounds and untreated garden trimmings can often go to a local compost or organics collection.

banana peelcoffee groundsgarden leaves

Check whether your local collection accepts compostable packaging; many do not.

02 · GETS A SECOND LIFERecyclableQuick cues+

Clean paper, cardboard, glass jars and many rigid plastic containers may be recyclable when accepted by your local program.

plastic bottlepaper & cardglass jar

Empty, clean and dry items; keep food, liquids and tangled bags out of the stream.

03 · HANDLE WITH CAREHazardousQuick cues+

Batteries, paints, solvents, pesticides and some cleaners need a designated household hazardous-waste or take-back service.

old batterypaint canchemical cleaner

Keep containers closed and labelled. Never pour chemicals down a drain or mix them together.

04 · RECOVER THE TECHElectronicQuick cues+

Phones, computers, cables and small appliances contain useful recoverable materials. Look for an e-waste drop-off or manufacturer take-back scheme.

smartphonechargersmall appliance

Remove personal data where possible and do not put lithium batteries in household recycling.
